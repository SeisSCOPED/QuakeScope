## 1. What is benchmarked, and against what

The 2026 catalogue is produced by PhaseNet (Zhu and Beroza, 2019) as
implemented in SeisBench (Woollam et al., 2022) with one of three weight sets
per campaign: `original` (Zhu and Beroza, Northern California) for the
western-states campaign, `jma_wc` (PhaseNetWC, Japan Meteorological Agency
data, double filter width) for the global campaign, and `obs` (PickBlue,
Bornstein et al., 2024, PhaseNet backbone) for ocean-bottom stations. Two more
weight sets enter the comparison: `quakescope2026`, a fine-tune of `jma_wc`
made for this project (v7 of Denolle-Lab/phasenet-retrain), and `instance`
(trained on the Italian INSTANCE dataset), the weight the 2025 campaign ran.

Five studies, five references:

| study | question | reference | tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| US sequences | which land weight, and does the fine-tune generalise? | manually reviewed SCEDC and NCEDC arrivals on curated ComCat event lists; four sequences 2003 to 2024 | 0.5 s |
| Global sequences | does the answer hold outside the US? | GeoNet, INGV and NOA analyst arrivals from their FDSN event services; Kaikōura 2016, Norcia 2016, Thessaly 2021 | 0.5 s |
| Ridgecrest aftershocks | dense overlapping events | SCEDC analyst arrivals, 21 events in 30 min | 0.5 s (0.25 to 2 s swept) |
| Ocean bottom | which OBS weight; do the stored picks agree with published analyst picks; caldera microseismicity | iasp91 predictions on three deployments; Barcheck et al. (2023) AACSE analyst picks; University of Washington Axial picks; LDEO ML-DD events | 10 s / 1 s / 0.5 s |
| Western reproduction | do the stored picks reproduce? | the campaign's own Parquet, re-picked through ObsPy/FDSN on another CPU architecture | exact match |

## 2. Protocol

**Reference construction.** In the US the earthquakes are fixed by curated
reviewed-event lists (`docs/rerun_2026/comcat_reviewed_*.csv`) and the
arrivals are harvested from every archive that located them, matched on origin
time and distance (2 s, 30 km), because ComCat carries one authoritative
solution per event and SCEDC and NCEDC locate the same earthquake with
different station sets. Only picks with `evaluation_mode == "manual"` are
kept. Abroad the reference is the operator's bulletin, harvested live from the
FDSN event service and cached (`tutorials/global_sequences_cache/`) so a
re-run scores against the same picks; GeoNet's automatic AIC picks are
excluded. AACSE uses Barcheck's published tables (doi:10.7298/01da-ka24);
Axial uses the UW HypoDD phase file and the LDEO ML-DD catalogue.

**Stations and windows.** Five stations per US sequence chosen nearest-first
among those that return data; six per global sequence chosen by analyst-pick
count, since at Kaikōura the reviewed picks sit 80 to 200 km out. Windows
start 10 min after the mainshock and run 30 min to 3 h depending on how much
of the sequence the analysts worked through.

**Inference.** Every weight set runs once per station with both thresholds at
0.02 and the peak probability of each pick is kept, so any threshold can be
applied afterwards without re-running. Headline tables use 0.3; the sweep uses
0.05 to 0.7. Waveforms come from the archives' public S3 buckets where they
exist (SCEDC, NCEDC) and from FDSN otherwise, the same sources the campaign
reads.

**Matching and metrics.** A reference pick is recovered if the model has a
pick of the same phase on the same station within the tolerance; the nearest
one is taken. Reported per sequence and phase: `analyst` (the reference count,
which is the sample size), `recall`, `MAE` and median bias of the residual on
matched picks, and `extra`, the model picks with no reference counterpart.
**Precision is not computed**: in a dense sequence most unmatched picks are
real arrivals nobody had time to mark, so they are reported as extra
detections and never as false positives.

**Matched pick budgets.** A shared threshold does not hold the operating point
fixed: the weights' probabilities sit on different scales and `original` emits
about twice the S picks of the others at 0.3. Recall is therefore also read at
equal numbers of emitted picks, by interpolating each model's sweep at the
midpoint of the budget range all models can reach. Where a model cannot reach
the others' counts with its threshold on the floor, that is reported as a
ceiling.

**Reproduction.** 52 station-days of the western campaign (a mainshock day and
a quiet day for five stations of each of four sequences) are re-picked locally
with the production modules imported from `sb_catalog.src`, reading the same
station-days over FDSN instead of S3, and compared to the stored Parquet on
(station, band, phase, peak) exactly, with no tolerance, so that any drift in
the data path shows.

## 4. Reading the results

**Recall of analyst picks is 0.6 to 0.9 for P and 0.4 to 0.9 for S at a
shared threshold, and the ordering of the land weights at that threshold is
mostly a matter of how many picks each emits.** `original` leads S on every
sequence at 0.3 because it emits about twice the S picks of the PhaseNetWC
pair; at a matched budget that lead is gone: it is the weakest of the four on
five of the six sequence-phases abroad and trades places with the PhaseNetWC
pair in the US. The fine-tune `quakescope2026` and its parent `jma_wc` sit
within three points of each other at matched budgets everywhere: the fine-tune
is ahead by one to two points on the US sequences and behind by up to three on
all three abroad, a mean difference of a few thousandths. Its timing edge
over the parent is 0 to 17 ms of mean absolute residual, largest on S in the
US and within 6 ms abroad. The fine-tune therefore adds nothing that survives
leaving the region it was checked on, and the global campaign stays on
`jma_wc`.

**`instance` is the most efficient weight wherever it can reach the budget,
and cannot always reach it.** At matched budgets it leads by 4 to 22 points
abroad and by 3 to 6 at San Simeon and Mendocino; at Ridgecrest, the densest near-field
sequence, it tops out at a third of the others' pick counts with its threshold
on the floor. That ceiling, not its calibration, is why the western campaign
runs `original`.

**Thresholds belong to the weight set.** The same 0.3 puts the four weights at
different points on the same recall curve, so a campaign that changes weights
and keeps the threshold changes its completeness without meaning to.

**Offshore, the OBS-trained weights lead the land weights by 5 to 15 points of
detection, and no single one wins everywhere.** The `obs` campaign's stored
picks recover 86 % of Barcheck's manual P and S at AACSE within 1 s, with
median residuals of 20 to 40 ms; the same weight on the network's land
stations gives 91 % and 85 %. Re-scored on the same windows, the stored
campaign pick, which had the whole day as context, beats a live 3-minute run
of the same weight (0.80 against 0.70 P at 0.3). At Axial Seamount the
campaign detects half of the M 0.5 to 1 events and almost none below M 0,
which is where most of that catalogue sits; the campaign's Axial picks should
not be read below M 0.

**The stored western catalogue reproduces.** On the station-days that hold
campaign picks, 99.97 % of the picks came back with the same phase, the same
peak time to the millisecond and the same confidence to float32 rounding from
a different data source, transport and processor; the rest are the same
arrivals one to three samples apart. What the reproduction study also found is
that 19 of 52 targeted station-days hold no campaign picks, and that several of
those had data: the silent skips in section 5.

## 5. Caveats that bound the claims

- **The reference is not exhaustive and not a pick archive.** A pick counts
  because an analyst made it and a location used it. Unmatched model picks are
  not false positives; deciding which are needs association across stations,
  which none of these studies does.
- **Sample sizes differ by an order of magnitude** between sequences (San
  Simeon: 16 analyst S picks; Norcia: 656). Read the `analyst` column with
  every recall.
- **Leakage favours the fine-tune and it still loses.** `quakescope2026`
  trained on a corpus that includes INSTANCE (Norcia is in-domain) and global
  sets that may hold Kaikōura and Thessaly; `jma_wc` is Japanese data only and
  is held out on every sequence by construction. `instance` is in-domain at
  Norcia. The three global sequences are kept as test assets and are to be
  excluded from any future training manifest.
- **v7 was selected by reading a benchmark across 19 fine-tunes**, with no
  held-out split, so its timing edge on that benchmark was expected to shrink
  on new data; here it is within 6 ms of its parent.
- **Thessaly S is capped near 0.5 for every weight**, a property of the
  reference or the data, cause not identified; do not quote it without saying
  so.
- **Two AACSE OBS stations score zero** because the archive's timing wanders by
  hours relative to what the analysts used (WD46, WD47; WS72 drifts from
  September 2018), and 5 % of the analyst arrivals fall on station-days the
  campaign never read.
- **Axial is a limit of the weight set on caldera seismicity**, not of the
  archive: S-P of 0.5 to 0.7 s, most events below M 0, data downsampled from
  200 to 100 Hz. The campaign's Axial catalogue should not be read below M 0.
- **Silent skips.** Station-days the campaign never read are recorded like
  empty days; three mechanisms are documented in the reproduction report and a
  fourth, a resumed shard overwriting its own first checkpoint files, in
  [`docs/rerun_2026/28_resumed_shard_overwrite.md`](../rerun_2026/28_resumed_shard_overwrite.md)
  (at most 11,582 station-day-channels in `western`, 0.15 % of those
  processed). The first three are not yet quantified for the whole catalogue.
- **Versions.** The benchmarks ran SeisBench 0.12.3; the campaigns ran
  0.12.5. The reproduction study shows the picks agree to float32 rounding
  across that difference, but the campaign-side comparison in the OBS study
  (stored pick against a live pick on the same window) mixes the two.

## 6. What remains before submission

1. Widen the reproduction sample to the EarthScope restricted path, which is
   most of the campaign by volume and cannot be re-picked from a laptop.
2. Quantify coverage for the whole western catalogue: the resumed-shard
   overwrite (bounded per shard by the checkpoint interval), the gap rule
   fixed on 2026-09-10 after western had run, and failed day listings.

   once it has picked NZ, HL and HT (section 8 of that notebook does it
   automatically).
4. An association-level benchmark on one sequence, so that extra detections
   can be classified.

   recovered.
